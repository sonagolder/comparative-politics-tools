Measures and falsifiability

A hypothesis is only worth testing when it is falsifiable (some finding could show it to be wrong). When you operationalize the concepts in your hypotheses and choose specific measures to represent them, it’s important to make sure that the claim you are testing remains falsifiable and that you didn’t accidentally create a tautology.

Take the coding rule that defines your measure, and read the hypothesis with the rule written out in place of the concept. If the thing the claim predicts is already part of the coding rule, then you won’t be able to find a way to falsify the claim.
