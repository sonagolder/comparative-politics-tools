Coding the same country three ways

Democracy is a concept. Before we can test theories about it, we must operationalize it by defining a rule that maps observable characteristics of a country onto a measure of democracy. Three widely used measures are Democracy-Dictatorship, Freedom House, and V-Dem, and their coding rules do not always assign the same score or category to the same country-year.

Here you are handed one country-year at a time and you code it under all three. The point is not that one of them is right. It is to see exactly where in the coding rules the three measures part company — what information each one uses from the record, what it ignores, and how it puts the pieces together.

Countries in practice mode are labeled by letter. The records are written for this tool; they are not real country-years, and the letters are there so that nothing you already believe about a particular country does the coding for you.

Two countries compared

These two are worth doing back to back, and in either order.

Democracy-Dictatorship

Four conditions. A country is a democracy if and only if all four hold; if any one fails, it is a dictatorship. Each condition is scored 1 or 0 and the four are multiplied.

    Freedom House

    Questions on political rights and on civil rights, each scored 0 to 4 by a country expert. Each set is summed, converted to a 7-point scale, and the two are averaged. Lower is more free. Freedom House asks ten political rights questions and fifteen civil rights questions; five of each are shown here so the arithmetic stays readable, and the conversion bands are adjusted to match.

    V-Dem — electoral democracy

    Five component indices ranging from 0 to 1. Three are derived from multiple expert assessments using V-Dem's Bayesian measurement model and include uncertainty intervals. Two are deterministic indices computed from institutional and legal information and therefore do not have uncertainty intervals. The components are combined twice — once by multiplying, once by adding with weights — and the two results are averaged.
