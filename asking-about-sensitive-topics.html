Asking about sensitive topics

Some questions people will not answer truthfully when asked directly: whether they took a bribe, sold their vote, or support an armed group. Chapter 7 describes several ways around this, and three of them are survey designs. Each design gives up something the researcher would normally have, any individual’s answer, in exchange for an honest estimate of how common the view or behavior is in the group as a whole.

Every case asks the same five things: which technique was used, how the design is put together, what the estimate is, whether any single answer still gives someone away, and whether a stated conclusion follows. Countries are labeled by letter so that what you already believe about a real place does not do the work. The one real survey, in the guided walkthrough, is identified as such.

Public officials

Surveys of people who work for the state, about things their employer or the law would punish.

Elections and parties

Surveys of voters, about how they were treated during a campaign and which political groups they favor.

Work, school, and neighborhood

Surveys about everyday conduct and attitudes that people have reasons to keep to themselves.
