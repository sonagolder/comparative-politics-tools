Interactive tools for Principles of Comparative Politics

Guided walkthroughs and practice exercises to accompany the fourth edition of Principles of Comparative Politics by Clark, Golder, and Golder (2024), organized by chapter. Each tool opens with a guided walkthrough; switch to practice mode when you are ready to work through cases on your own.

Chapter 2

  • Valid and invalid arguments Tell valid arguments from invalid ones using the chapter’s set diagram, and see what testing a theory can and cannot show.
  • Necessary and sufficient conditions Find the one observation that would refute a claim of necessity or sufficiency, and tell a claim from its converse.

Chapter 3

  • Solving extensive form games Solve game trees by backward induction and write out the subgame perfect equilibrium, including games with more than one.
  • Inferring power from behavior Start from what happened in the exit, voice, and loyalty game and work out what can be concluded about who held power.

Chapter 4

  • Finding best responses Find best responses, dominant strategies, and Nash equilibria in strategic form games.
  • Leaving the state of nature Adjust the parameters of the state of nature and civil society games and compare what each game predicts.
  • Changes to the balance of power Place countries by state power and society power, and see how the same change moves countries that start in different places.

Chapter 5

  • Coding the same country three ways Code a country-year under Democracy-Dictatorship, Freedom House, and V-Dem, and account for where the three disagree.
  • Measures and falsifiability Check whether a hypothesis can still be falsified once a concept is replaced by the rule used to measure it.

Chapter 6

  • Economic development and democracy Read the ruler’s dependence and an economic actor’s exit option off a described economy, and predict what the exit, voice, and loyalty game says happens.

Chapter 7

  • Asking about sensitive topics Randomized response, list experiments, and endorsement experiments: how each design works and what it can estimate.

Chapter 8

  • Solving incomplete information games Solve games in which one player does not know which world Nature chose, and find the critical probability that decides what that player does.
  • Collective action theory Read Table 8.1 one scenario at a time to find your best response, then change the group and the stakes and see why people who all want a public good may not act to get it.
  • Tipping models Work out how big a protest gets from each person's revolutionary threshold, and see why revolutions come as a surprise.

For instructors

  • Chapter, figure, box, and problem numbers in the tools refer to the fourth edition (2024).
  • Each tool is a single web page. It runs in any current browser on a computer or phone, with no login or installation, and it stores nothing about the people who use it.
  • Link to this page or to any individual tool from your syllabus or course site. Each tool’s address stays the same when it is updated.
  • The tools are designed for practice and self-study. Because the answers are built into each page, they are not suited to graded work.
