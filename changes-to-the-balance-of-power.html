Changes to the balance of power

The chapter ends with three possibilities for state behavior — absent, unconstrained, and constrained — determined by the relative balance of power between the state and society. Acemoglu and Robinson set them out as regions of one space, with a narrow corridor running between them.

These aren’t distinct types of states. As the chapter puts it, they’re simply descriptions of different forms of state behavior. What varies isn’t the state’s nature but the balance it finds itself in.

Each case gives you a country and a change — some arriving in a year, some over centuries. The same change doesn’t have the same consequence everywhere: it’s not possible to make unconditional predictions about the effects of structural changes on state behavior.
