Selectorate theory

If every leader wants to stay in power, why do some govern well and others steal? Selectorate theory answers with two numbers: the size of the winning coalition and the size of the selectorate. This tool works through the chapter’s example of two societies, lets you change the numbers yourself, and then gives you calculations and regimes to work on.

Practice

2. What is the chance that a member of the selectorate ends up in a winning coalition — and so the chance that a defector makes it into the next leader’s coalition?

3. What is the most a member could expect in private goods by defecting to a challenger?

4. The leader pays each member just over what defecting is worth. Out of each member’s possible share, how much can the leader keep?

5. Which would give each member of the winning coalition more: spending all the revenue on private goods, or all of it on public goods?
