Economic development and democracy

What matters in the exit, voice, and loyalty game is whether the ruler depends on the economic actor and whether the economic actor can put its assets beyond the ruler’s reach. Chapter 6 argues that we can think about things like natural resource wealth, foreign aid, and the state’s role in the economy in terms of whether the ruler is autonomous or dependent and whether the economic actor has a credible exit option. Here you are given a description of an economy and asked to assess it along these lines.

The game is not re-solved here. Its four solutions arrive as given, from the chapter.

Two points before you start. First, L and E are properties of a relationship, not of a country: a ruler can depend on financiers and be indifferent to farm laborers in the same year, and the model then gives two different answers about the same place. Every case therefore names the economic actor it concerns. Second, exit means putting productive assets beyond the ruler’s reach — hiding them, idling them, or taking them out of the country. Economic actors who exit need not go anywhere themselves.

Aid does not make a ruler autonomous in general. It moves the dependence onto the donor, so these cases are a different game with a different second player — the ruler and the aid donor, not the ruler and the citizenry. The outcome table changes with them.
