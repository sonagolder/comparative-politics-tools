Winsets in two dimensions

A committee is deciding two issues at once, so a policy is a point in a two-dimensional space. Each member has an ideal point and prefers policies that are closer to it. Both issues matter equally to every member, so a member is indifferent between all the policies the same distance away.

That makes each member’s indifference curve with respect to the status quo a circle, centered on their ideal point and passing through the status quo. Anything inside the circle is preferred to the status quo. The winset of the status quo is every policy that a majority prefers to it: here, the places where at least two circles overlap.

Accompanies Chapter 10 of Principles of Comparative Politics, 4th edition (Clark, Golder, and Golder 2024). The committees, ideal points and policies are illustrations written for this tool; none is taken from the chapter or its problems.
